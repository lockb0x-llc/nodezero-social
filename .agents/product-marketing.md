# NodeZero Social — Product Marketing Context

_Auto-drafted from codebase. Update as the product evolves._

## 1. Product Overview

**One-liner:** NodeZero is the social network you actually own — your data lives in your Solid Pod, not our servers.

**What it does:** NodeZero is a decentralized social platform that lets people connect, post, and discover locally without surrendering their data to a platform. Your profile, connections, and posts live in a Solid Pod you control. Your identity is anchored on Stellar via a zero-knowledge attestation so NodeZero can verify you're a real human without learning who you are.

**Category:** Decentralized social network / Web3 social / privacy-first social app

**Product type:** Consumer app (web + mobile via Expo), open source, free tier via solidcommunity.net

**Business model:** Open source; Pod hosting via third-party providers (solidcommunity.net, inrupt, etc.); future: premium relay/Pod hosting

---

## 2. Target Audience

**Primary:** Privacy-conscious individuals frustrated by surveillance capitalism in social media; crypto-native users active in the Stellar and Web3 ecosystems.

**Secondary:** Developers and builders in the Solid/decentralized web community; hackathon participants (Stellar Hacks).

**Psychographic:** They've read about data harvesting and feel uneasy. They want to stay connected but not at the cost of their digital identity. They're curious about Web3 but want apps that actually work, not just wallets.

**Jobs to be done:**
1. Stay connected with people nearby and in my circles without feeding an algorithm.
2. Own my social graph so I can take it with me if a platform dies or changes its terms.
3. Verify my identity online without handing over my real name, location, or email.

---

## 3. Core Problem

**The problem:** Every major social network owns your identity. They sell your attention, sell your data, and can delete your account — and your social graph — at any time.

**What it costs them:** Years of connections gone the moment you leave. No portability. No privacy. No recourse.

**Emotional tension:** Feeling trapped — you know the platform is bad, but all your people are there.

---

## 4. Solution & Differentiation

**Core insight:** Your social graph should live with you, not inside a company's database.

**How NodeZero is different:**
- Data lives in a user-controlled Solid Pod (W3C standard), not NodeZero's servers.
- Identity is anchored on Stellar with ZK attestations — NodeZero can verify you're real without learning who you are.
- Feed is strictly chronological — no engagement farming, no recommendation algorithm.
- Local discovery via H3 hexagonal grids — find real people nearby, not global influencers.
- You can delete your NodeZero account and keep everything. Your Pod, your data.

## 6. Positioning Statement

For privacy-conscious individuals and Web3 natives who are tired of surrendering their data to social platforms, NodeZero is the decentralized social network that stores your identity and connections in a personal Solid Pod you control — so you own your social graph permanently, and no company can take it from you.

## 7. Brand Voice

- **Direct.** Say what it is. Skip adjectives that don't add meaning.
- **Confident.** Own the positioning — we're not "kind of like Twitter but decentralized." We're fundamentally different.
- **Technical but not exclusive.** The underlying tech (Solid, Stellar, ZK) is real, but users shouldn't need to know the names to feel the benefit.
- **Principled, not preachy.** State what we believe and let users decide. Don't lecture.

---

## 8. Onboarding Aha Moment

**New user aha moment:** The first time a user sees their own profile URL pointing to their own Pod (e.g., `alice.pod.nodezero.social/profile/card`) and realizes: "This is mine. NodeZero doesn't own any of this."

**Activation steps:**
1. Create Solid Pod (or connect existing one)
2. Complete wallet-linked attestation (NodeZeroIdentity + Lockb0x on Stellar)
3. Post first broadcast to Local Grid

**Returning user job:** Sign in and see their feed exactly where they left it — no algorithm-driven "while you were away" wedge.
